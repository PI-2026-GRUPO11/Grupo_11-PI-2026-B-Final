Chronicle

Worlds that pass us by

Every day, I cross paths with hundreds of people. On the bus, in the school hallways, in lines and on the streets. We share spaces, small experiences that go unnoticed, and then we go our separate ways, almost always without giving it much thought.

Without thinking that each of these people carries an entire story within themselves. There are stories we will never hear, memories we will never know and traditions we may never fully understand. There is a word for this perception: sonder.

The term describes the realization that every person has a life as complex as our own, full of experiences we do not know about.

Every person carries traits. On their face, in their hair, on their skin, in the way they speak, walk, eat. They carry an entire ancestry: grandparents, great-grandparents, peoples, languages and religions.

This is what makes society so rich, so complex and so beautiful. Every face that passes by us bears marks of some place in the world, of some people. Every accent, every seasoning, every way of praying or celebrating is a heritage that remains alive, often without us noticing.

Brazil is a living example of this. Here, Indigenous, African, European, Asian and Arab cultures met, transformed each other and continue to leave their marks on one another. There is no single Brazilian face. There is an infinite variety of them. There is not just one culture, but many, coexisting and transforming over time.

Sonder is realizing all of this. It is recognizing that every person carries their own stories, achievements, defeats and experiences — as different from ours as they are equally important.
